Why this exists

The case reads like real law. It isn't.

In Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y. 2023), two attorneys filed a brief built on cases a chatbot invented, and were sanctioned for it. A fabricated case isn't a public fact a fact-checker can look up. Only the firm's own cite-check can catch it.

Para is that cite-check, at the moment it matters: between "the AI drafted it" and "the partner relied on it." A paralegal at a five-person firm gets the protection of a dedicated cite-checking team.

How it decides

Four signals. The strictest one wins.

Every memo is checked four ways at once. Para's own code makes the call, never an AI model: agents add evidence and can only make a decision stricter.

1

Your firm's citation lists

Every case, short name and bare reporter cite is matched against the cases your firm has verified and the ones it knows are fake. Wrong volume, year, party or holding is caught.

Ezio fact-check

Trust tier, tone, bias and agreement, plus a fact-check of public claims such as what an amendment says. A contradicted claim blocks.

✓ Approve

Every check passes. The memo goes to the partner, and only then.

‖ Hold

A case on neither list, advocacy, a weak score, an unfinished placeholder, or any check that couldn't finish. An attorney logs a reason, then proceeds or rejects.

✕ Block

A fabricated or misstated case, or a claim Ezio contradicts. It can never be pushed through; fix it and resend.

  • Fails closed. If Ezio or an agent is down, slow or unreadable, the memo is held, never sent unread.
  • Agents can't lower a decision. An AI saying "this case is fine" never overrides your list. A memo can't talk its way through.
  • Every call is audited. Input, evidence, reason, reviewer and whether it was sent, in an append-only log.

The agents

Three Hermes agents on HarnessRouter, one job each.

Each agent is a configured harness with its own instructions, tools and limits. They are split by job and permission: the two that judge can't write, and the one that writes never judges.
